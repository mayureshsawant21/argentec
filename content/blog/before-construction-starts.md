Every building project has a moment when drawings turn into concrete. The decisions made before that moment are cheap to change. The ones made after it are not.

This is sample content for the blog layout. Replace it with a real article before the site goes live.

## 1. Is the scope written down in one place?

Architects, consultants and the client often hold slightly different versions of the scope. A single scope document, agreed by all of them, removes most of the arguments that come later.

- What is included, and what is explicitly excluded
- Which specification each package follows
- Who approves a change, and how quickly

## 2. Has the site been fully investigated?

Soil reports, existing services and access routes all shape the foundation design and the logistics plan. Surprises found during excavation are the most expensive kind.

## 3. Is the budget tied to quantities?

A budget built from measured quantities can be checked as the design develops. A budget built from a rate per square foot cannot.

> A schedule is only as reliable as the decisions it assumes have already been made.

## 4. Does the schedule show the critical path?

The programme should make clear which activities hold up everything else, so that the team knows where a day lost really costs a day.

## 5. Who coordinates the services?

Structural, mechanical, electrical and plumbing drawings need to be checked against each other before work starts. Clashes resolved on paper cost a fraction of clashes resolved on site.

## Closing the loop
